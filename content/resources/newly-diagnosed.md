## You don't have to do everything today

A new food allergy diagnosis can feel overwhelming. Your house doesn't have to
be transformed by tonight; you don't have to memorize every label tomorrow.
This week, focus on a small number of things that genuinely matter, and let
the rest come later.

## Day 1-2: Get the emergency plan in writing

1. **Fill in your emergency action plan** with your allergist. The
   one-page form lists symptoms and exactly when to use epinephrine. Print
   two copies (one for the fridge, one for your bag).
2. **Pick up two epinephrine auto-injectors.** You always carry two. They go
   together, in the same bag.
3. **Practice with a trainer device.** Auto-injectors come with a trainer
   that has no needle and no medication. Practice once on a pillow so the
   motion feels familiar.

## Day 3-4: Read your kitchen

You don't have to throw everything out. Spend 30 minutes:

- Read the labels on the foods you currently have. Look at the **ingredient
  list** and any **"Contains:"** line. (In the U.S., the top 9 allergens
  must be declared in plain language.)
- Set anything that contains your allergen aside. Decide as a family
  whether you want to keep those items at home (in a separate area) or pass
  them on.
- Don't worry about "may contain" or "made on shared equipment" lines yet.
  Ask your allergist next visit how strict to be about those.

## Day 5-6: Tell two or three key people

You don't owe everyone an explanation. Start small:

- Anyone who feeds your child regularly (grandparents, daycare,
  babysitter, a close friend's family). Show them the action plan.
- Your child's school or preschool. Ask what forms they need, and whether
  there's a 504 plan, an Individual Health Plan, or both.
- Anyone who lives in the house. Make sure everyone knows where the
  auto-injectors live and how to use them.

## Day 7: Take a breath

A diagnosis is a beginning, not an emergency in itself. Most days will be
ordinary. The plan, the auto-injectors, the people who know &mdash; that's
the foundation. Everything else (label-reading shortcuts, restaurant
strategies, school party planning) builds from there over weeks and months,
not days.

## What's next

Once the basics are in place, these guides walk you through the rest:

- Birthday party game plan
- School forms checklist
- Grocery shopping and label reading
- Travel with food allergies
- Talking to grandparents and caregivers

Take them one at a time, in the order that fits your life.
