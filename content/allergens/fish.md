## The basics

Fish allergy is allergic reaction to finned fish — salmon, tuna, cod,
tilapia, halibut, anchovy, and others. It's biologically distinct from
shellfish allergy (crustaceans and mollusks). The two often co-occur but
not always.

Many people allergic to one fish react to multiple species because the main
allergenic protein, **parvalbumin**, is conserved across species. Some
patients tolerate certain fish (often tuna or salmon) while reacting to
others — testing is the only way to know.

Fish allergy is more often an adult-onset allergy than other Top-9
allergens, and it tends to be **lifelong**. Most patients don't outgrow it.

## Reading labels

FALCPA requires the **specific fish** to be named — "salmon," "tuna,"
"cod," "anchovy," etc. — not just "fish."

Watch for these less-obvious sources:

- **Anchovy** in sauces and dressings
- **Surimi**, **imitation crab**, **kamaboko** — whitefish-based
- **Fish stock**, **fish broth**, **dashi** (Japanese, often bonito-based)
- **Caviar and fish roe**
- **Worcestershire sauce** (most contains anchovy)
- **Caesar dressing** (anchovy)
- **Many Asian fish sauces** — Vietnamese nuoc mam, Thai nam pla,
  Filipino patis, Chinese yu lu

## Hidden sources

Fish appears in:

- **Caesar dressing**
- **Worcestershire sauce** (most brands, with rare exceptions)
- **Asian fish sauces** in stir-fries, marinades, and dipping sauces
- **Surimi** and imitation crab in California rolls, seafood salad, and
  some kamaboko-style snacks
- **Pizza** (anchovy on Caesar pizza, puttanesca pizza)
- **Bouillabaisse, paella, cioppino, and other seafood stews**
- **Some omega-3 supplements** (fish oil; algae-based alternatives exist)
- **Some pet foods** — not a direct exposure but a cross-contact concern
  in shared kitchens

## Daily life

**At home:** Many fish-allergic households decide whether to keep fish in
the house at all. If so, dedicated cookware, surfaces, and storage help
prevent cross-contact.

**At school:** Tuna sandwiches and fish sticks are the most common school
exposures. Coordinate with the cafeteria for lunch alternatives.

**Eating out:** Sushi restaurants, seafood-focused restaurants, and Asian
restaurants (because of fish sauces) are highest-risk. Even non-seafood
items can be cross-contacted from shared grills and fryers. Always ask
about anchovy in sauces.

**Travel:** Fish is foundational to many Mediterranean, Asian, and Nordic
cuisines. Pack a chef card.

## Treatments

There is no widely available oral immunotherapy for fish. **Omalizumab**
(Xolair) was approved in 2024 for multiple food allergies; ask your
allergist whether your fish allergy fits the criteria.

For most fish-allergic patients, the management plan is **strict avoidance,
two epinephrine auto-injectors, and a current emergency action plan**.

## Questions to ask your allergist

- Which specific fish species do I need to avoid? Are some fish safe?
- Should I also avoid shellfish? (Often the answer is "no, get tested
  separately.")
- Am I a candidate for omalizumab?
- Are fish-oil supplements safe for me, or should I use algae-based
  omega-3?
- What's my threshold for using epinephrine after accidental exposure?
