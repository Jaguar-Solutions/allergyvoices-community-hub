## What changed

In January 2020, the FDA approved **Palforzia** — brand name for AR101, a
peanut-flour-based oral immunotherapy product — for children ages 4-17
with confirmed peanut allergy. It was the first FDA-approved treatment
for peanut allergy.

The original PALISADE trial showed that after roughly 6-12 months of
escalating daily doses, most children could tolerate at least 600 mg of
peanut protein (about 2-3 peanuts) without reacting — enough to protect
against accidental exposure.

Five years later, real-world data from clinical practices is starting to
clarify what families should actually expect.

## Who this affects

Palforzia is FDA-approved for **children ages 4-17 with peanut allergy**.
A pediatric application for ages 1-3 has been pursued and may expand the
approved age range; check current status with your allergist.

Importantly, Palforzia and "OIT" are not the same thing. OIT (oral
immunotherapy) is a class of treatments using gradually increasing doses
of an allergen. Palforzia is a specific FDA-approved product with a
standardized peanut flour. Many specialty allergy centers offer
**non-Palforzia OIT** using actual peanuts, peanut butter, or peanut
flour from other sources. Both work; they have different cost,
convenience, and supervision profiles.

## What real-world data is showing

Several studies and registries since 2020 have published outcomes from
Palforzia patients in clinical practice (as opposed to controlled
trials):

- **Effectiveness**: Most patients who complete the dosing schedule
  reach a maintenance dose that protects against accidental exposure.
- **Discontinuation**: Real-world drop-out rates have been **higher than
  in the trials** — often 20-30% over the first year. Common reasons:
  GI side effects, dosing fatigue, and (less often) significant allergic
  reactions.
- **Side effects**: Mild-to-moderate GI symptoms (stomach pain, nausea)
  are common, especially during dose escalation. Most resolve with dose
  adjustment.
- **Severe reactions**: Rare but real. Patients on Palforzia carry
  epinephrine auto-injectors throughout treatment.
- **Quality of life**: Mixed. Some families report dramatic reductions
  in anxiety; others report the daily dosing schedule itself as a
  burden.

## What families should know

- **Palforzia is a long-term commitment**, typically several years of
  daily dosing once maintenance is reached. Stopping the daily dose can
  cause tolerance to fade.
- **Cost varies widely.** Insurance coverage has improved since 2020 but
  still has gaps. Out-of-pocket costs can be significant if not covered.
  The manufacturer offers patient assistance programs.
- **It's not a cure.** Most families describe the goal as "bite-proof"
  — protection against an accidental nibble — rather than freely eating
  peanut.
- **Daily compliance matters.** Missing doses can cause loss of
  tolerance and increase reaction risk on the next dose.
- **Monthly clinic visits during escalation.** The first 6-12 months
  involve regular trips to the allergist for supervised dose increases.
- **Avoid factors that increase reaction risk on dosing day:** intense
  exercise within 2 hours of dose, illness, hot showers, or empty
  stomach.

## How Palforzia compares with other paths

Three current paths for peanut allergy management:

| Approach | Goal | Time commitment | Cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Strict avoidance + epinephrine** | Avoid reactions | Daily vigilance, no clinic visits | $0 beyond auto-injectors |
| **Palforzia OIT** | Bite-proof tolerance | Daily dose, monthly visits during escalation, multi-year | Insurance + copays |
| **Omalizumab (Xolair)** | Raise reaction threshold | Monthly injection, ongoing | Insurance + copays |
| **Non-Palforzia OIT** | Bite-proof or higher tolerance | Daily dose, periodic clinic visits | Varies (often higher out-of-pocket) |

Many specialty centers now combine omalizumab with OIT to make the
escalation phase faster and safer. Ask your allergist about combined
protocols if you're considering OIT.

## Editorial note

Palforzia is a real treatment with real benefits and real costs. The
choice between OIT, omalizumab, and continued strict avoidance is
personal, depends on your child's reaction history, your family's
schedule, and what you're hoping to achieve. The right next step is a
conversation with an allergist who specializes in food allergy
treatment.
