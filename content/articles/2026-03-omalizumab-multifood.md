## What changed

In **February 2024**, the U.S. Food and Drug Administration approved
**omalizumab** (brand name Xolair) for reducing the risk of allergic reactions,
including anaphylaxis, that may occur with accidental exposure to one or more
foods. It's the first medication approved for **multiple** food allergies at
once.

Omalizumab is not new — it's been used for years to treat severe asthma and
chronic hives. The food allergy approval is based on the **OUtMATCH** clinical
trial, which showed that participants taking omalizumab were able to tolerate
significantly higher amounts of peanut, milk, egg, and cashew before reacting
than those on placebo.

## Who this affects

The FDA approval covers adults and children **ages 1 and older** with one or
more IgE-mediated food allergies. The medication is given by injection, usually
every 2-4 weeks, and it doesn't replace allergen avoidance.

Importantly: **omalizumab is not a cure.** It raises the threshold for many
people, but it doesn't eliminate the allergy, and protection wears off when the
medication is stopped. Families still need to:

- Avoid trigger foods
- Carry two epinephrine auto-injectors
- Follow their emergency action plan

## What families should know

- **It's a long-term commitment.** Once started, the medication needs to be
  continued to maintain protection.
- **The first dose is given in a clinical setting.** Allergic reactions to
  omalizumab itself are uncommon but possible, including anaphylaxis.
- **Insurance coverage varies.** Specialty pharmacy benefits and prior
  authorization are typically required. Ask your allergist's office about
  copay assistance programs.
- **It works alongside OIT, not instead of it.** Some allergists are using
  omalizumab to make OIT escalation faster and safer; others see it as an
  alternative path. Your allergist can talk through which approach fits your
  family's situation.

## Editorial note

This summary is educational and not medical advice. Decisions about
omalizumab should be made with an allergist who knows your child's full
history.
